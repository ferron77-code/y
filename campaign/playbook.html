El Movimiento

El Sol De Cuba lands 14 September. Then a licensed Miami signal becomes a podcast, and the podcast becomes a coalition.

Nearest deadline

El Sol De Cuba — 14 September

Three weeks. The date is fixed and personal to Tony, so everything else moves around it. Two things on this timeline cannot be recovered if they slip, and both fall inside the next fortnight.

The Spotify pitch closes 7 September — permanently

Editorial playlist pitching through Spotify for Artists shuts seven days before release. Miss that window and you cannot pitch this song at all — not late, not at all. It is one form, it takes twenty minutes, and it is the single highest-leverage action in this whole plan.

The distributor needs the track by 31 August

Most distributors take 7–14 days to push a release to every platform, and the track needs to be live in Spotify's system before you can pitch it on the 7th. If it is not already delivered, that is this week's job — nothing else on this page matters if the song is not on the shelves on the day.

One scheduling note worth a conversation. The 14th is a Monday. The industry releases on Friday because Spotify's Release Radar refreshes then — a Monday release waits until the following Friday to reach it. You lose one algorithmic push.

Keep the date anyway. A release day that means something to the person who wrote the song gives you a story to tell, and the story is worth more here than one playlist refresh. Just know the tradeoff, and lean harder on the 990 AM rotation and the guest network in week one to make up the difference.

The unfair advantage

You already own the hard part

Almost every podcast launches into silence. It has no audience, no distribution, and no reason for a guest to say yes. It spends its first year begging for both.

You are not launching a podcast. You are converting a broadcast audience into a movement.

990 AM is a licensed signal in the largest Cuban-American market in the United States, and Tony owns it. That single fact rewrites the entire strategy, because it changes what you are asking people for. You are not asking an influencer to promote your band. You are offering them a slot on real radio.

That is a rare thing to be able to give away, and it is the whole engine of what follows. Everything in this plan runs on it.

The exchange

Why they say yes

Stop thinking of this as influencer marketing. Think of it as booking. The ask is come on the show — and the promotion follows on its own, because guests share their own appearances without being asked. A booking is a gift; a promo request is a favour. Only one of them scales.

You give

A broadcast slot

Airtime on an FCC-licensed Miami station. Not a Zoom call posted to YouTube — actual radio, which still carries weight with this audience and their parents.

You give

Assets they own

A produced episode plus cut vertical clips, handed over. Most guests leave a podcast with nothing they can post. Yours leave with a week of content.

You get

Their audience, once

They post the episode because it flatters them to. No contract, no fee. Track it — the share rate is the number that tells you if the show is worth their time.

Format

Three segments, built from the brand

The name already contains the structure. B459 is a date — before and after. Build the show on it so the format teaches the brand every single episode.

SegmentRunsThe question
Antes10–12 min The guest's Cuba — family story, what was lost, what got carried out. This is the archive the movement exists to build.
Ahora15–18 min What they are working on now. This is their payment: uninterrupted time to talk about their own project.
Después6–8 min One concrete thing the next generation should do. Short, quotable, and the natural clip.
Cierre3–4 min Close on a track from the catalogue. The music is the outro, never an ad read — that is how it becomes part of the show instead of an interruption.

Record bilingual. Tony's radio audience skews Spanish-dominant; the generation you are trying to reach is English-dominant. A show that only picks one is throwing away half the reason it exists.

Sequence

The launch, in four phases

These are ordered because the order matters. Phase 2 fails without Phase 1's inventory, and Phase 3 has nothing to prove without Phase 2's numbers.

01

Foundation

Four weeks before launch
  • Site live on the real domain, streaming links in, socials claimed on every platform in the follow row.
  • Press kit: Tony's bio, show description, cover art, three sample questions, technical requirements. One PDF, one link.
  • Start Meta advertiser verification now. Cuba policy is classed as social-issue advertising and verification takes days — begin it before you need it, not when a launch ad is waiting.
  • Book the first ten guests from Tony's own network. Thirty years and twenty-four stations is a rolodex most shows would kill for. Cold outreach comes later.
02

Record in the dark

Weeks 1–4 · nothing published yet
  • Record six episodes before releasing a single one. Resist publishing early.
  • Two reasons: the format will be wrong in episode one and you want to fix it before anyone hears it, and a new listener who finds you needs a back catalogue to binge or they never return.
  • Cut clips as you go. By launch you should have forty short-form assets sitting ready.
03

Launch on a date that means something

Week 5 · one day, everything at once
  • Publish three episodes simultaneously, not one. Every guest promotes on the same day, so the signal stacks instead of spreading thin.
  • Anchor it to a tentpole date from the calendar below. January 1 is the one the brand is literally named after.
  • Run it hard on 990 AM the same week — promos in your own inventory, which costs you nothing.
04

Flywheel

Week 6 onward · weekly
  • One episode a week, same day, without exception. Consistency beats production value at this stage.
  • Now open Tier 2 and Tier 3 outreach — with published episodes, real download numbers, and named guests, the pitch is evidence instead of a promise.
  • Every music release becomes its own tentpole, with the artist as that week's guest.
Outreach

Three tiers, three different jobs

Do not treat these the same. They convert at different rates, they want different things, and mixing them up wastes the only currency you have, which is Tony's and Pedro's time.

100k+ · book 2–3 a quarter

Tier 1 — Anchor

Nationally known voices. Low reply rate, gatekeepers, slow. You are not booking them for their audience — you are booking them so everyone in Tier 2 says yes faster. They are social proof, not reach.

10k–100k · the bulk of bookings

Tier 2 — Core

Journalists, musicians, academics, mid-size creators. Best ratio of reply rate to reach anywhere in the pipeline. This tier is the show. Spend most of your outreach hours here.

Under 10k · volume

Tier 3 — Community

Micro-creators, exile-community organisers, local Miami figures. Highest reply and share rates, most engaged audiences, and genuinely delighted to be asked. The cheapest real reach you will ever buy.

Cadence is three touches: the invitation, a follow-up after five business days, a final note a week later. Then stop and mark them no-response. A fourth chase does not convert — it just costs you the relationship for when you have something better to offer.

Scripts

What to actually send

Short, specific, and one clear ask. The most common failure is a long message that explains the whole movement and never says what it wants. Replace anything in gold with the real detail — a message that could have been sent to anyone gets treated like it was.

DM · first touch

Hi [name] — I produce a show on 990 AM in Miami about Cuba before and after '59, hosted by Tony Calatayud.

Your [specific post/piece and why it stuck] is exactly the conversation we want on air. Would you come on? It's 40 minutes, remote is fine, and you'll get the clips to post yourself.

Happy to send details if you're open to it.

Guardrails

Things that can actually bite

Do not scrape Instagram for emails

It breaks Meta's terms, and bulk-mailing harvested addresses gets your sending domain flagged — right at the moment you need email reaching people. Use published business contacts: the Email button on professional accounts, link-in-bio press kits, YouTube About pages, newsroom directories. Those are addresses people put up because they want to be reached.

Meta treats Cuba as a social issue

Paid promotion touching Cuba policy needs advertiser verification and a "Paid for by" disclaimer before it will run. Verification takes days and involves ID. Start it in Phase 1 — not the week of launch.

Marketing email has rules

Any promotional email needs a real physical mailing address and a working unsubscribe, honoured within ten business days (CAN-SPAM). Use the station's address. A one-to-one booking invitation you wrote yourself is not a blast — keep it that way and the rules stay simple.

Sponsored airtime needs identifying

Anything aired on 990 AM in exchange for payment or consideration needs on-air sponsorship identification. Tony knows this territory better than anyone here — just make sure whoever books the promos does too.
